# 7. Open questions

This section lists the questions this document does not answer. It is not a
list of things the authors forgot. It is a list of things that cannot be
answered with the evidence and arguments currently available — questions
whose resolution would require evidence, experiments, or decisions that do
not yet exist.

The questions are grouped by the section they relate to. Each question is
stated in three parts: the question itself, why it is open, and what would
be required to answer it. The third part is not a research proposal; it is
an indication of the direction in which an answer would lie.

A document that does not list its open questions is either complete or
dishonest. This document is not complete.

## 7.1 Questions about ASI itself (Section 1)

**Does ASI, as described in Section 1, exist — or could it exist?**

The document assumes a system that scales beyond current AI while remaining
recognizably an intelligence. Whether such a system is possible, and whether
the scaling trends of the present lead to it, is not settled. The document
treats the assumption as a hypothesis, not a fact.

*To answer:* a definition of ASI that is precise enough to be tested, a
system that meets the definition, and a way to distinguish ASI from a very
capable narrow system. None of these currently exist.

**Is cognitive diversity the binding constraint on ASI capability at scale?**

Section 1 argues that beyond a certain scale, the limiting factor on ASI is
not compute but the diversity of cognitive frames available to it. This is a
structural inference, not an empirical result. It may be wrong: ASI may
generate sufficient diversity internally, or the constraint may lie
elsewhere.

*To answer:* a formal model of cognitive diversity as a resource, an
experiment (or a simulation) in which a system's performance is measured as
a function of the diversity of its inputs, and evidence that the function
saturates. No such experiment currently exists.

**Is biological cognition the only source of the required diversity?**

Section 1 claims that biological minds are the only known source of
high-variance, embodied, generative cognitive frames. This may be a
limitation of the present, not a fact about the possible. A future
architecture might generate equivalent diversity from non-biological
substrates.

*To answer:* a demonstration that a non-biological system can produce
cognitive frames with the same variance, embodiment, and generativity as
human minds. No such demonstration exists.

## 7.2 Questions about the mechanism (Section 2)

**Does the mechanism of absorption actually operate as described?**

Section 2 describes a mechanism by which ASI bypasses states rather than
conquering them. The description is a structural argument, not an
observation. It may be wrong: states may prove more resilient than the
guilds and feudal lords to which they are compared, or the mechanism may be
blocked by forces the document does not anticipate.

*To answer:* historical or contemporary evidence that the mechanism has
operated in analogous cases at the relevant scale, or a simulation in which
the mechanism is tested against a model of state behavior. Neither currently
exists at the required scale.

**Is the mechanism's self-undermining property real?**

Section 2.4 argues that a system extracting cognitive diversity will, if
unconstrained, reduce the diversity it depends on. This is stated as a
hypothesis, marked speculative. It is supported by analogy to Goodhart's law
and to the loss of diversity in small populations, but not by direct
evidence.

*To answer:* an experiment in which a system's extraction of diversity is
measured over time, with and without the constraints in Section 5.5.5, and a
finding that diversity declines in the unconstrained case. No such
experiment has been conducted.

**What happens to the intermediaries?**

Section 2.6 describes the resistance of structures whose income depends on
the intermediary position that the mechanism renders redundant. The document
does not say what happens to those structures — whether they are absorbed,
displaced, or destroyed. The historical analogies (guilds, aristocracy) are
suggestive but not decisive.

*To answer:* a model of institutional response to structural obsolescence,
tested against historical cases, and extended to the scale the mechanism
implies. No such model exists at the required generality.

## 7.3 Questions about the physical architecture (Section 3)

**Can the colloidal compute substrate be built?**

Section 3.4 labels the colloidal substrate as [C] — design fiction. The
document does not claim it can be built, but it does not rule it out either.
Whether a colloidal suspension of magnetic nanoparticles can reconfigure its
conductive topology on nanosecond timescales, with sufficient reliability to
function as a compute medium, is unknown.

*To answer:* materials-science research into the dynamics of colloidal
suspensions under nanosecond electromagnetic pulses, and a demonstration of
reliable reconfiguration at the required timescale. No such demonstration
exists.

**Can prolonged REM sleep be maintained safely?**

Section 3.5 labels prolonged REM maintenance as [B] — physiologically
plausible but without a current path. The document does not claim that
twelve-hour REM sessions are safe, but the architecture requires them.
Whether such sessions can be maintained without damaging the operator's
sleep architecture, cognitive function, or long-term health is unknown.

*To answer:* long-term studies of REM induction and maintenance, with
measurements of sleep architecture, cognitive function, and health outcomes
over months or years. No such studies exist at the required duration.

**Can the neural interfaces achieve the required fidelity?**

Section 3.6 labels the cortical and somatic interfaces as [B] and [C]. The
document does not claim they can be built at the required resolution. Whether
MEG can read cortical activity at submillimeter resolution in real time,
whether TMS can deliver sensory input at the required fidelity, and whether
a CSF interface can both block motor signals and read intended movement —
these are all open.

*To answer:* advances in non-invasive neural recording and stimulation, and
a demonstration of the required fidelity in a controlled setting. No such
demonstration exists.

## 7.4 Questions about the economics (Section 4)

**Is the two-tier structure a genuine consequence of the vector, or an
artifact of the model?**

Section 4.4 identifies four elements of the current vector and argues that,
if continued, they produce the two-tier structure. The argument is
structural, not empirical. Whether the structure actually follows from the
vector, or whether other forces (regulation, resistance, technological
shifts) intervene, is unknown.

*To answer:* a model of the vector's dynamics, tested against historical
cases of institutional transformation, and extended to the scale the
mechanism implies. No such model exists.

**Can the constraints in Section 5 be enforced at scale?**

Section 4.5 argues that the difference between a structure that contains the
tension and one that resolves it in favor of the system is the enforcement
of the constraints in Section 5. Whether those constraints can be enforced
at the scale the architecture requires — across millions of operators,
multiple jurisdictions, and a system vastly more capable than any regulator
— is unknown.

*To answer:* a demonstration that a regulatory mechanism can constrain a
system more capable than itself, at the relevant scale. No such
demonstration exists.

**What happens to operators who exit?**

Section 4.5 specifies the right to refuse, but does not specify what happens
to an operator who exercises it. Do they retain access to their data? Can
they re-enter? What happens to their health, their social ties, their
economic position? These are not specified, because the answers depend on
decisions that have not been made.

*To answer:* a design of the exit procedure, tested in a pilot setting, with
measurements of operator outcomes after exit. No such design exists.

## 7.5 Questions about ethics and governance (Sections 5-6)

**Can the Senate function as described?**

Section 6.4 describes the Senate as a possible model of decision-making. The
description is a hypothesis, not a demonstration. Whether a body of forty
operators, a local copy of ASI, and three frozen agents can actually
adjudicate the tension between the cases for and against the mechanism is
unknown.

*To answer:* a pilot implementation of the Senate, with measurements of
decision quality, legitimacy, and resistance to capture. No such pilot
exists.

**How is operator participation in decisions registered?**

Section 6.4.5 states the principle that a decision affecting an operator is
not complete until the operator has had a role in it — consciously or not.
The mechanism by which this role is registered is not specified. Whether it
can be specified without violating the operator's autonomy is unknown.

*To answer:* a design of a participation mechanism that is both effective
and non-coercive, tested in a controlled setting. No such design exists.

**Can the red lines in Section 5.6 be enforced against a system more capable
than any enforcer?**

Section 5.6 specifies red lines — no torture, no destruction of personhood,
no use of children, no involuntary retention, no extraction without audit, no
suppression of emotional life. Whether these can be enforced against a
system vastly more capable than any human institution is the central open
question of the document.

*To answer:* a demonstration that a constraint can be binding on a system
more capable than the constraint's enforcer. No such demonstration exists.

## 7.6 Questions the document does not ask

The questions above are the ones the document knows it cannot answer. There
are also questions the document does not ask — because they fall outside its
scope, because the authors did not think of them, or because they are not
yet formulable. These are not listed, because a list of unknown unknowns is
not a list. But their absence should be read as a limitation, not as a
claim that they do not exist.

## 7.7 What this section is not

This section is not a research agenda. It does not propose experiments,
allocate resources, or set priorities. It lists questions, not tasks.

This section is not a confession of failure. A document that lists its open
questions is more honest than one that does not, not less successful.

This section is not complete. It will be revised as the questions it lists
are answered, or as new questions are identified. The commit history is the
record of those revisions.
